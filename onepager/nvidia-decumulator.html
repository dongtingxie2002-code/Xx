Single-stock disposal programme

Selling down NVIDIA, a little above the market — every day.

A simple way to trim a large NVIDIA holding gradually over the next quarter, at a fixed price meaningfully above today's — for no upfront cost. In exchange you accept two clear rules. Here is exactly how it works, and what it is worth.

01In one breath

You own NVIDIA shares and would like to reduce the position — but not dump it all at once, and ideally at a better price than today's. This note sells 1,000 of your NVIDIA shares every trading day at a fixed $252.76 (about 14% above today) for up to three months. Two conditions shape it: (1) if NVIDIA ever closes 5% lower, at $210.70, the programme simply stops and you keep the rest of your shares; (2) on any day NVIDIA closes above $252.76, that day you sell double — 2,000 shares — still at $252.76. There is no fee and nothing to pay upfront.

03How it works — three rules

  • Sell daily at a premium. Each trading day the programme sells 1,000 of your shares at $252.76, ~14% above where NVIDIA started — regardless of where it trades that day.
  • If it dips to the floor, you're done. The first time NVIDIA closes at or below $210.70 (−5%), the programme ends. You keep every share not yet sold. The floor is switched off for the first week, so the programme always runs at least five days.
  • If it pushes higher, you sell double. On any day NVIDIA closes above $252.76, that day's clip doubles to 2,000 shares — still at $252.76. This is the trade-off for the high sale price: you part with more stock just as it runs away from you.

04What could happen

Across thousands of simulated paths, roughly 3 in 4 programmes end early (NVIDIA touches the floor) and about 1 in 4 run the full quarter. Three illustrative paths:

It eases back

~46% chance · ends early, lower

NVIDIA drifts down and touches $210.70 — on average after about three weeks. The programme winds down having sold a slice of your stock at the enhanced price.

Typically sells ~16,500 sh at $252.76 (~$4.2m), then stops — you keep the balance. You exited part of the position ~14% above today.

It treads water

~9% chance · runs full term

NVIDIA stays in the $210.70–$252.76 corridor for the whole quarter. You quietly sell your full intended size, every day, at the premium price.

Sells ~80,000 sh at $252.76 (~$20m). The ideal result — your entire programme done ~14% above today's market.

It rallies hard

~25% chance · finishes above strike

NVIDIA powers through $252.76 (averaging ~$289 at maturity). You sell double on the strong days and exit a large size — but below where the stock ends.

Sells ~68,000 sh at $252.76 (~$17m). You still banked +14% — but had you simply held, those shares would be worth more. This is the real cost.

The $252.76 level is solved, not quoted: it is the highest sale price at which the bank breaks even, so the note costs nothing. NVIDIA's high volatility is precisely what funds such a generous-looking premium — the two rules hand back that value.

  • You give up the big upside. The single largest risk. If NVIDIA keeps climbing you sell double at $252.76 the whole way, missing the rest of the move. You should be comfortable selling at $252.76 and view further upside as a bonus you're willing to forgo.
  • The premium isn't free money. It is fair compensation for those two rules — on average the outcome is worth exactly today's market, not more.
  • Early end on weakness. If NVIDIA falls you stop selling and keep shares that are now worth less — though selling was your aim, so this is rarely the worst outcome.
  • You stay invested while it runs. Unsold shares keep their full market exposure, up and down.
  • Counterparty & liquidity. An over-the-counter note with the issuing bank; not listed, and unwinding early is at the bank's price.

06Is it right for you?

✓ It may suit you if…

  • You want to reduce NVIDIA over the coming months, not in one block.
  • You'd be happy to sell at $252.76 (+14%).
  • You think a further sharp rally is unlikely near-term.

✕ Think twice if…

  • You are bullish and want full upside — just hold, or sell calls instead.
  • You need certainty on exactly how many shares you'll sell.
  • You may need the cash on a fixed date — the programme can end early or run on.
