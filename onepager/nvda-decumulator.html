3-Month Daily Decumulator on NVIDIA Corp. (NASDAQ: NVDA)

You hold roughly 120,000 NVIDIA shares — about $26.6m, and the single largest line in the family book. You've said you'd like to trim it into strength rather than sell it all at once. This note does exactly that, automatically: a little each day, at a price 14% above where NVIDIA trades today — and it costs you nothing upfront.

In one breath

Every trading day for three months, you sell a slice of NVIDIA at a fixed $252.75 — +14% above today's $221.79. You always sell at that price, never lower. Two conditions shape it: if NVIDIA rallies above $252.75 you sell double that day, and if it falls 5% to $210.70 the programme simply stops and you keep the rest of your shares.

How it actually works
  1. Each trading day, the bank buys 500 NVIDIA shares from you at $252.75 — fixed for the whole quarter, whatever the market is doing.
  2. On any day NVDA closes above $252.75, you sell 1,000 shares instead of 500 (the "2× gearing"). You still get $252.75 — but you've sold more while it's rising.
  3. If NVDA closes at or below $210.70 (−5%), the programme knocks out: it ends there, no more selling, and you keep every remaining share.
  1. A short 5-day grace window at the start means the floor can't end the deal in the first week.
  2. There is no upfront premium and no fee embedded in the levels — the terms are struck at fair value.
  3. At maturity (or knock-out) it's simply done: you've sold whatever shares the path delivered, all at $252.75, and settled in cash.

The honest trade-off

This is not free money — and we won't pretend it is. Because the note is struck at fair, zero-cost value, the 14% head-start is paid for by the two conditions: selling double into a rally, and stopping early in a sell-off. On a pure mathematical expectation they roughly offset.

What you are really buying is certainty and discipline: a guaranteed above-market exit on whatever shares trade, and a structured, unemotional way to reduce a position you've already decided is fully valued. You never sell below today's price. The thing you give up is the home-run — the scenario where NVDA doubles and you'd rather have held it all.

Method. The enhanced sale price is solved by Monte-Carlo simulation (300,000 risk-neutral price paths, daily steps, geometric-Brownian dynamics) so the present value of all embedded cashflows — daily sales at the strike, 2× gearing above it, and the −5% knock-out — nets to zero at inception. At those terms the fair strike is $252.75 (+14.0% vs spot, +12.9% vs the 3-month forward). Across simulations the note knocks out ~75% of the time (expected ~day 22) and decumulates ~17,600 shares on average (5th–95th percentile: 2,500–54,500).
